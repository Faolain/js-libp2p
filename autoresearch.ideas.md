- New diagnostics narrowed the 0.24.x fault line further and retired several stale ideas. Gating node-side sends on the actual `open` event and adding a small post-open delay were both worth testing, but neither was sufficient. A minimal two-channel polyfill repro on aggressive `0.32.1` can open the negotiated handshake channel plus one application channel and successfully deliver a single `16 KiB` message, so the newer stack is not simply unable to create one post-handshake channel. But once that same minimal repro starts **repeated** application-channel sends, it fails before delivering any app bytes (about `16 MiB` sent with `mtu=1500`, about `8 MiB` with default/unset MTU) even without libp2p framing, while the paired `0.29.0` repro cleanly transfers `64 MiB`. Follow-up trigger mapping has now ruled out explicit `enableIceTcp` as the sole cause, and has shown something much stronger: disabling **server-side ICE UDP mux** alone makes that smaller repeated-send repro on `0.32.1` transfer the full `64 MiB` successfully under otherwise aggressive settings. That makes server-side UDP mux the clearest trigger candidate in the newer repeated-send regression surface, even though the full libp2p benchmark still needs additional conditions to become stable. The best next path is therefore to focus on what server-side mux changes in the 0.24.x line (candidate type/path, socket ownership, buffering semantics, or close behavior) and/or package the small mux-sensitive repro for upstream node-datachannel/libdatachannel investigation.
